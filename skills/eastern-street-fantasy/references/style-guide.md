# Design and Review Guide

## Derive structure from the motif

Identify the action or ability most worth showing, then derive equipment placement, shape, and opening mechanisms from the character's tools and movements. Personality, species, or abilities can also define a character; a specific occupation is not required.

Choose one dominant shape and let the others support it. A broad upper body might contrast with slender legs, or layered draping might surround a compact torso. Headwear, sleeves, shoes, and equipment need not all be exaggerated. Mechanical structures can strengthen contrasts between slender and bulky, hard and soft; organic characters can establish the same relationships through clothing and protective gear.

Build streetwear into the garment itself. Changing sleeve volume, closures, fabric panels, and graphic scale is more effective than adding sneakers to a classical robe. East Asian influences should likewise have a structural basis rather than relying solely on dragon motifs, lettering, or conical hats. A hybrid design is not a historical costume reconstruction.

## Organize color and detail

Allocate the main color fields before adding patterns and materials. Dark areas can separate bright colors and stabilize the silhouette, but a dark base is not mandatory. Muted palettes can retain a clear graphic language through contrasts in value and area.

Choose thematic patterns from abstract occupational symbols, graffiti, stripes, checkerboards, or other suitable forms. Traditional motifs are valid when adapted to the design's color fields and scale. Avoid substituting intricate surface ornament for structural design.

Distinguish large graphics from high detail density. A bold leaf motif can still read as a clear color field; clustered fasteners, stitching, and mechanical parts increase the local reading effort. Place readily legible shapes beside concentrated detail rather than requiring flat, blank regions.

Render materials to clarify structure. Distinguish fabric, metal, and plastic through edges, folds, and selective highlights rather than equally strong reflections or excessive microtexture across the whole figure.

## Observable review criteria

| Criterion | Effective result | Signs to revise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silhouette | Dominant shape and volume contrast remain visible at thumbnail size | Accessories merge into a cluttered mass; every part competes for attention |
| Theme | Key shapes, equipment, or actions communicate the character concept | Characters can only be distinguished by names and explanatory text |
| Fusion | East Asian garment structures and streetwear components jointly determine the cut | Classical clothing with graffiti pasted on, or generic armor with a conical hat |
| Color | Large fields have a hierarchy of area and value | Many hues occupy equal areas with no clear focal point |
| Density | Patterns read as large shapes; fine structures have local emphasis | Every surface carries the same density of detail |
| Structure | Body parts, joints, and prop contact points connect clearly | Extra fingers, broken limbs, or purposeless tubing |
| Edit consistency | User-specified identifying features remain intact and requested changes are clear | Identity, build, or core equipment changes merely to fit the style |

Use these criteria to locate specific problems, not to declare success through mechanical scoring. Separate style choices from generation defects: organic bodies, different palettes, or user-requested realistic proportions are not inherently flaws. For multiple views, also check consistency in garments, equipment placement, and anatomy. A polished single image does not establish readiness for production modeling.
