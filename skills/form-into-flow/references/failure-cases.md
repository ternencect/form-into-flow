# Common Failure Cases

Use these patterns to review structural problems in people, animals, and other subjects. They describe defects, not generation recipes.

| Failure | Appearance | Misleading improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uniform fragments or heavy paint | Subject and background share rough texture everywhere, with no continuous quiet area | Material presence does not establish spatial organization |
| Smaller but detached subject | An intact realistic person or animal sits on a pale background | More open space does not integrate subject and surroundings |
| Added gauze-like ribbons | Clothing or ground extends into uniformly thick decorative membranes with complete outlines | Direction and transparency do not establish structural transformation |
| Realistic animal on an ink base | A fully rendered animal sits above separately added ink lines and color layers | Removing heavy paint may leave the pasted-on effect intact |
| Local improvements hide overall failure | Identity, open space, and texture improve separately, but subject and environment still read as two layers | Individual gains cannot substitute for a unified space |

Revise the current design when these defects appear. Do not infer that an entire subject category is unsuitable, ban all gradients, curves, or texture, or claim that one prompt term caused a result based on a single attempt.

Review overall relationships using the [style guide](style-guide.md). Do not turn failed methods into style targets or expect a longer list of exclusions to repair the composition automatically.
